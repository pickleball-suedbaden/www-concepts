# Pickleball Südbaden e.V. · Terminology and voice guide

German (DE) is the default language of pickleball-suedbaden.de. English (EN) is the second language. This guide sets the words, tone and formats for both, so that every page, email, flyer and post reads as one club.

- Every table has a DE column and an EN column. A FR column can be added to each table later.
- Where an English string is quoted, it is the wording the site uses now.

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 How we address people

| Situation | DE | EN |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors, players, members | **du**, always lower case: du, dich, dir, dein (also in emails) | you |
| Organisations: halls, councils, schools, companies, sponsors | **ihr**, euch, euer: „Eure Halle hat die Linien schon.“ | you |
| The reader speaking to the club (consent boxes, quotes) | ihr: „Ihr dürft Fotos von mir verwenden.“ | you |
| Legal texts: Impressum, Datenschutz, Satzung | Impersonal, with no direct address | Impersonal |
| SEPA mandate | Fixed standard wording, first person („Ich ermächtige …“) | English courtesy translation under the German text |
| Buttons | Infinitive, verb last: „Platz sichern“, „Mitglied werden“ | Verb first: "Save my spot", "Become a member" |
| Imperatives in text | Short form: „wähl“, „füll“, „schreib“, „bring“. Keep the -e where the short form is hard to say: „öffne“, „rechne“. | Plain imperative |

### 1.2 Tone

- **A friendly club host.** Warm, precise and lightly funny, never at the reader's expense.
- **Practical first.** If you remove the joke, the line must still give the date, place, price or next step.
- **Short sentences, one idea each.** German runs 20–35 % longer than English, so cut words, not information.
- **Calm on serious topics.** Child protection, insurance, money, personal data and legal text carry no jokes.
- **Honest about status.** Write „ausstehend“, „folgt“ or „Beispiel“ instead of guessing.
  - Never call membership fees tax-deductible.
  - Never imply insurance, charitable status or federation membership before it is confirmed.
- **Few exclamation marks.** Use at most one per screen.
- **Avoid:**
  - „Seniorensport“;
  - "fastest-growing sport" claims without a source;
  - medical claims;
  - mocking tennis or padel;
  - celebrity endorsements.
- **EN:** plain international English in British spelling (organise, colour, neighbour, programme). Avoid US-only idioms in core copy.

### 1.3 Gender-inclusive language (DE)

Apply these rules in order.

1. **Neutral wording first.** Options:
   - plural participles: Mitspielende, Teilnehmende, Studierende, Ehrenamtliche;
   - collective or role nouns: Team, Vorstand, Spielleitung, Kursleitung;
   - „alle, die …“;
   - rephrasing around the person: „Wer neu ist, spielt erst mal mit anderen Neulingen.“
2. **The colon form, only where no natural neutral form exists.** Examples: „Trainer:in werden“, „Kontoinhaber:in“, plural „Trainer:innen“.
   - Avoid colon forms with articles or adjectives („ein:e neue:r“). Rephrase instead.
   - Screen readers read the colon as a short pause.
3. **Never the generic masculine for groups of people** („Spieler“, „Anfänger“, „Teilnehmer“).
   - Also do not use pair forms, asterisks, underscores, capital-I forms or slash forms. The site uses one system throughout.

**Words that are already neutral:** Mitglied, Gast/Gäste, Neuling, Person, Mensch, Leute, Team, Verein, Vorstand, Fans.

**Organisations** (partner companies, halls, councils) keep their usual nouns: „Gründungspartner werden“, „Zahlungsempfänger“. This rule is about people, not organisations.

|---|---|---|

| beginners, first-timers | Anfänger, Einsteiger | Neulinge, alle, die neu sind, „zum ersten Mal dabei“ |

| students | Studenten | Studierende |
| trainees | Azubis | Auszubildende |
| retirees | Rentner, Senioren | Leute im Ruhestand, Menschen im Ruhestand |

### 1.4 Humour and wordplay

**Rules:**
- Write the German line for German readers. Do not translate the English joke.
  - If a pun does not exist in German, find a German one.
  - If there is none, keep the plain sentence.
- Every pun sits next to the plain information it plays on.
- Jokes go in headlines, eyebrows and small microcopy. They never go in rules, forms, prices or legal text.

| EN line | DE line | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Badische Küche, now with a net. | Badische Küche, jetzt mit Netz. | Baden's food culture meets the kitchen zone. Always follow it with the plain kitchen explanation (section 7). |
| Three countries. One net. | Drei Länder. Ein Netz. | Translates one to one. |
| Doubles needs four. We saved you a spot. | Doppel braucht vier. Dein Platz ist frei. | Natural German, with no gendered nouns. |
| Easy to learn. Hard to stop. | Leicht gelernt. Schwer zu lassen. | Keeps the leicht/schwer contrast and the rhythm. |
| Eight to eighty. Same court. | Acht bis achtzig. Ein Feld. | Keeps the alliteration. |
| Nobody here plays "their age". | Hier spielt das Alter keine Rolle. | A German idiom with a built-in play on „spielen“. |
| Stay in the rally. (newsletter) | Bleib am Ball. | A German idiom that already lives in ball sports. |
| Join the club. | Willkommen im Club. | The German idiom carries the same wink. |
| No passport control. Just a shoe check. | Keine Passkontrolle. Nur Schuhkontrolle. | Rhymes in German. |
| Theory: done. Practice: Thursday? | Theorie: erledigt. Praxis: Donnerstag? | Translates one to one. |
| Zero experience. Zero euros. | Null Erfahrung. Null Euro. | Translates one to one. |
| My tennis elbow and I are very happy here. | Mein Tennisarm und ich fühlen uns hier sehr wohl. | „Tennisarm“ is the everyday German word. |
| Our hall partners love clean floors, and we love our hall partners. | Unsere Hallen lieben saubere Böden, und wir lieben unsere Hallen. | Avoids a people noun entirely. |
| the odd bad pun | ab und zu ein schlechter Wortwitz | Plain and self-mocking. |

**German-only extras** (use in DE only, with no EN equivalent):
- „Sauer macht lustig. Pickleball auch.“
- „eingelegt“ for losing 11–0.
- „Netzwerken am Netz“.
- „Dinken & Trinken“ as a strapline under the name Dink & Drink. The name itself stays English.

**Avoid in DE:**
- „Dein erstes Mal“. It is a common innuendo. Write „dein erster Termin“.
- Kitchen jokes with gendered images („Ab in die Küche“).
- „Gurke“ or „Gurkentruppe“ about people.
- „baden gehen“ (to flop).
- Puns that only work in English (dill, dink different, in a pickle).

### 1.5 Regional touches

We are a Freiburg club writing for Baden, Alsace and Basel. Use standard German that Swiss and Alsatian readers understand, plus a light local flavour.

**Rules:**
- Use at most one regional touch per section.
- It must be understood without local knowledge, or be explained in the same line.
- Never use a regional touch in instructions, forms or legal text.
- No sentences in dialect spelling, and no jokes at other regions' expense.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Baden food and drink | Badische Küche, Apfelschorle, Flammkuchen („das Gericht, das Baden und das Elsass beide für sich beanspruchen“) |
| A Baden word for a get-together | „Hock“: „Dink & Drink, unser Pickleball-Hock nach dem Open Play“ |
| The tri-border area | Dreiländereck (Basel, Lörrach, Weil am Rhein), Oberrhein |
| Landscape | Schwarzwald, Kaiserstuhl, Rhein, Baar, Bodensee |
| Freiburg | Bächle; „Freiburg im Breisgau“ in full where Swiss readers might think of Fribourg |
| Greetings to neighbours | „Bienvenue · Grüezi“, and the name tag band „Hallo · Bonjour · Hello“ |
| Words that work across borders | „Tram“ rather than „Straßenbahn“ or „ÖPNV“ (Swiss readers say ÖV); „magst du …?“ |

**Place names:**

| EN | DE |
|---|---|
| Black Forest | Schwarzwald |
| Upper Rhine | Oberrhein |
| Lake Constance | Bodensee |
| Alsace | das Elsass („im Elsass“, „aus dem Elsass“) |

## 2. Programme and feature names

### 2.1 Programmes and features

Programme and feature names stay in English in every language. They are proper names, so do not translate or decline them. Everything around them is German.

- **Article:** use the article of the German head noun. Examples: „das Open Play“ (das Spiel), „das First Serve“ (das Schnuppertraining, so „Nächstes First Serve“), „die Kitchen School“ (die Schule), „die Paddle Library“, „die Level Ladder“, „die First 100“, „ein Pickle Pop-up“, „der Founders’ Cup“.
- **In compounds,** hyphenate: „First-Serve-Termin“, „Open-Play-Abend“.
- **Generic phrases stay lower case and German:** „vom ersten Aufschlag bis zur Mitgliedschaft“ (not First Serve).

| Name | DE description | EN description |
|---|---|---|
| **First Serve** | Unser kostenloses Schnuppertraining: 90 Minuten mit Spielleitung und Leih-Paddle, für alle, die noch nie gespielt haben (Level 0). | Our free taster: 90 minutes with a host and a loan paddle, for people who have never played (Level 0). |
| **Kitchen School** | Einstiegskurs über vier Wochen, ein Abend pro Woche: vom ersten Ballwechsel bis zum ersten Open-Play-Spiel (Level 0–1). | A 4-week course, one evening a week: from first rally to first open-play game (Level 0–1). |
| **Open Play** | Regelmäßige Spielabende nach Level: Doppel in wechselnden Paarungen, für Mitglieder und Gäste. | Regular sessions by level: doubles with rotating partners, for members and guests. |
